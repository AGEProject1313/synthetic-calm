Where Did They Go?

July 8, 2023 · By Giulia Verra

Questions surrounding missing construction workers at the Veyra development site.

The Beacon archive image. File reference: veyra-construction-site-2023.png

Over the past year, hundreds of workers have passed through the construction site of the ambitious Veyra urban development project.

Drivers. Welders. Concrete workers. Electricians. General laborers.

A project of this scale could hardly exist without them.

Yet speaking with residents from nearby communities over the last several weeks, I repeatedly encountered the same unsettling question: where did some of them go?

Where did some of them go?

The concern does not stem from any official report. There are no confirmed disappearances. No active investigations. No public allegations of wrongdoing.

And yet, stories continue to circulate.

A former worker claims that several men employed in a northern construction sector abruptly stopped returning to their accommodations.

A local resident recalls a relative who allegedly left for work at the site and never returned home.

Another witness insists that a workplace incident occurred during the early phases of construction, followed by the sudden removal of several workers from the area.

None of these claims could be independently verified.

There are no names. No documents. No photographs. No employment records available to this newspaper. No police reports connecting the stories together.

Only fragments. Rumors. Half-finished accounts passed from one person to another.

Under normal circumstances, that would be enough to dismiss the matter entirely. Perhaps it still is.

Large construction projects often attract speculation. People leave jobs unexpectedly. Memories become distorted. Stories grow with each retelling.

But if there is nothing behind these accounts, why do so many of them sound so remarkably similar?

Several residents interviewed by The Beacon described seeing unmarked vehicles entering restricted construction zones during nighttime hours.

Could those vehicles have belonged to private contractors? Almost certainly. Could they have been performing entirely legitimate work? Of course.

Yet when asked about specific security arrangements connected to the site, representatives involved with the project declined to provide details, citing operational confidentiality.

Perhaps there is no story here. Perhaps every witness is mistaken. Or perhaps there are questions that nobody has seriously tried to ask.

Editor’s Note: The Beacon was unable to independently verify several claims described in this article. Readers should treat witness testimony with appropriate caution until further evidence becomes available.
