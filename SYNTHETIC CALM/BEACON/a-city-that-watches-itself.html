Veyra Files

A City That Watches Itself

May 22, 2024 · By Giulia Verra

Monitoring systems, access badges and digital governance in Veyra.

The Beacon archive image. File reference: veyra-surveillance.png

Veyra's promise depends on integration. Access, mobility, services and public safety are all designed to speak the same language.

Its administrators call this efficiency. Civil rights advocates may eventually call it something else.

The city's badge system does more than open doors. It records movement. It defines permission. It turns civic life into a managed sequence of authorized states.

Adrian Veil, the public face of the project, frames this as liberation from disorder. He may be sincere. That is not necessarily comforting.

A city that watches itself can protect its residents. It can also train them to stop asking who is watching the watchers.
